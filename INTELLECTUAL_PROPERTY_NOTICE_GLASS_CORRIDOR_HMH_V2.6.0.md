### Author's Metacognitive Interpretation

This statement is positioned in Version 2.6.0 as the author's **metacognitive orientation and normative horizon**, not as an empirical finding. The purpose of HMH is to keep the rapid expansion of AI-assisted technical reasoning connected to the question of what human purposes, human dignity, human agency, peaceful coexistence, and long-term planetary sustainability are intended to serve.

The project therefore treats **Humanity Matters Horizon (HMH)** as a higher-order orientation for the research trajectory: technical capability, AI-generated complexity, optimization, and engineering sophistication remain means; they do not become the ultimate criterion of research value. The metacognitive gate is consequently required to ask not only *Can this be solved?* or *Can this be optimized?*, but also *Does solving or optimizing it remain aligned with the humanitarian and peaceful purpose of the research?*

HMH is deliberately presented as an **author-defined normative/metacognitive horizon**, not as a validated scientific theory or a claim that humanity has a single universally agreed value system. Its role is to provide directional coherence for cooperative human–AI co-evolution while leaving empirical, ethical, legal, and institutional questions open to examination.

## 1A. HMH AND RESEARCH GOVERNANCE

HMH is added as an author-defined normative/metacognitive horizon. It does not override empirical evidence, humanitarian law, aviation safety requirements, cybersecurity standards, or legitimate institutional authority. Its governance function is directional: technical optimization must remain subordinate to the preservation of human agency, peaceful cooperation, humanitarian purpose, and sustainable long-term outcomes.

The project may use experienced engineering judgment to classify many familiar technical problems rapidly. However, this work does not establish that every technical challenge is automatically low-cost or readily solvable.

Engineering judgment is a prior, not empirical proof.
